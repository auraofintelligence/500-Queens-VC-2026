500 Queens / Second Skin Space Suits

Second Skin Space Suits

Design spacewear around the movement, fit and dignity of many different bodies.

The need worth working on.

A 'second skin' space suit is a demanding engineering ambition. The practical starting point is understanding how garments and equipment interact with real people: different heights, bust sizes, body mass, mobility, reach and ways of dressing. A suit concept cannot assume every wearer shares a single athletic shape.

What the enterprise could offer.

Build an inclusive fit and movement research studio for future spacewear. Start with non-pressurised mock-ups, not protective equipment. Explore fastening, adjustment, layering and the ability to complete tasks independently, then hand a traceable requirements brief to experienced suit engineers.

People to build with

  • Human-factors researchers and specialist garment designers
  • Space training teams exploring task ergonomics
  • Women whose bodies are poorly represented in conventional equipment sizing

A movement and fit workshop

01

Recruit consenting adult participants with varied body shapes and access needs.

02

Let participants choose practical tasks and describe existing equipment frustrations.

03

Make adjustable non-protective mock-ups and test reach, dressing and comfort.

04

Record participant feedback without publishing personal measurements.

05

Ask a qualified human-factors specialist to translate findings into design requirements.

The first result

A participant-reviewed fit brief and non-protective garment demonstrator.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Engineering development

The starting evidence

Human-factors and garment studies can inform a future suit; the pilot would not establish pressure protection or flight readiness.

The open question

No material, pressure system, thermal design or qualified engineering partner is specified in the original concept.

The next measurement

Compare task completion and wearer-reported fit across the agreed mock-up variations.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • A garment designer and human-factors lead
  • Paid participant involvement and accessible workshop space
  • A specialist space engineering reviewer

A possible business model

Contract fit research and development services could precede any specialised garment product.

A leadership decision to practise

Give wearers authority over what comfort and independence mean. Ensure larger and large-busted women influence the initial design, rather than being added as an afterthought.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • A comfortable mock-up may not work as a pressure garment.
  • Personal body measurements need careful participant control.
  • A glamorous illustration can conceal unresolved fit and engineering questions.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Would a large-busted, fat or differently mobile woman have equal influence over the suit design?
  • Can wearers choose privacy around body measurements and fitting?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
