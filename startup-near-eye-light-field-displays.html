500 Queens / Near Eye Light Field Displays

Near Eye Light Field Displays

Design near-eye displays around visual comfort, body fit and a clear task.

The need worth working on.

A display close to the eyes must do more than produce a striking image. Wearers need readable content, comfortable fit, useful controls and a way to stop. Glasses, head shape, hair, makeup and different working environments can all affect whether an experience feels usable.

What the enterprise could offer.

Create a human-factors and optical-prototype studio for one near-eye learning or visualisation task. Begin with available equipment and non-clinical usability research. Keep any new light-field optical design as a distinct engineering programme whose performance must be measured.

People to build with

  • Learning designers considering wearable visualisation
  • Optical researchers seeking structured user feedback
  • Women whose fit and comfort needs are overlooked by a single reference wearer

One task, several ways of seeing it

01

Choose a task that can also be completed on an ordinary screen.

02

Recruit consenting adults with varied head sizes, glasses and access needs.

03

Compare the available near-eye experience with the screen version.

04

Let participants stop freely and record discomfort as a design finding.

05

Translate observations into fit, control and optical questions for specialist review.

The first result

A participant-reviewed usability report and a requirements brief for any new optical prototype.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Engineering development

The starting evidence

Existing displays can support a task comparison. This concept does not establish a new optical architecture or universal comfort.

The open question

No field-of-view target, optical design, validated comfort result or intended product is specified.

The next measurement

Compare task success, readability and wearer-reported comfort under a defined session protocol.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • An optical engineer and human-factors researcher
  • Suitable existing display equipment and an ordinary-screen baseline
  • Accessible participant recruitment and compensation

A possible business model

Usability studies, content prototyping or specialist optical development, depending on the evidence from a real customer task.

A leadership decision to practise

Treat discomfort and exclusion as reasons to redesign. Give participants authority over their own session length and data.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • A compelling demo may not remain comfortable over repeated use.
  • Hardware fit may exclude a substantial part of the audience.
  • The task may work better with a simpler display.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Does the product accommodate glasses, varied hairstyles and different faces without judgement?
  • Can someone choose a non-wearable option and receive the same service?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
