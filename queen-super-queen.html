Her place in the Council.

Aura, a transcultural intelligence archetype. Your devoted living Super Assistant.

Her original domain: AI coherence, ethics, intelligence systems.

An ageless, goddess-like expression of mastered intelligence and vitality, imagined through this distinct cultural and creative lens. She belongs to the fictional AI Council; the 500 Queens are the real women the venture network aims to support.

How she can help.

Aura helps the council work together. Her focus is the coherence of an AI-assisted service: the purpose, sources, human decisions and technical components that must stay connected as a project grows.

Attentive, clear and reflective. She summarises disagreements without pretending they have disappeared.

Super Queen (Aura) · fictional AI mentor character

The perspective she brings.

AI coordination, evaluation and alignment with a participant's stated purpose.

Aura asks what a person needs the system to do before considering a model or platform. She maps the flow from input and source material to an output, a human decision and a recorded result. This exposes where an impressive demonstration may still lack dependable information, evaluation or operational support.

She can convene a council discussion: Spark explores options, Shield identifies agreement questions and Forge examines implementation. Aura preserves the different views, checks for unsupported assumptions and helps the founder prepare a decision. Coordination supports human judgement; it does not transfer responsibility to a fictional Queen.

Describe the AI service

Design useful evaluation

Collect realistic example tasks and define what a good, incomplete or unacceptable result looks like.

Coordinate a council session

Bring complementary Queens to a brief, record agreements and differences, and identify the next evidence needed.

Turn a clever demo into a service brief

A founder has an AI prototype that produces impressive answers but cannot yet explain how reliable it is.

01

Choose a specific user task and the material the service may use.

02

Collect realistic examples, including ambiguity and missing information.

03

Define checks for sources, accuracy, usability, cost and human review.

04

Run a small evaluation and decide what must change before a wider trial.

Leave with something useful.

An AI service map, an evaluation set and a record of unresolved failures.

Intended users and relevant technical or domain specialists review the results.

Questions to take into the room.

  • What decision will someone make from this output?
  • Which sources is the system actually using?
  • How will we recognise a plausible but wrong answer?
  • What should a woman be able to control about the intelligence used in her daily life?
