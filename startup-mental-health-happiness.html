500 Queens / Mental Health & Happiness

Mental Health & Happiness

Study what people say would improve their wellbeing without reducing happiness to a sales score.

The need worth working on.

A business can collect satisfaction ratings while missing loneliness, insecure work, poor access or the pressure of unpaid care. People also mean different things by happiness. A useful research service should let participants define a good outcome and resist turning distress into a simple market segment.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop a wellbeing service-research practice using non-clinical interviews, service mapping and participant-chosen indicators. Focus on one setting, such as a shared workplace or community programme, and identify a practical change that the responsible organisation can actually make.

People to build with

  • Community organisations improving a programme
  • Workplaces examining a practical barrier to participation
  • Women-led ventures testing a non-clinical wellbeing offer

A participant-defined improvement

01

Ask a willing group what makes the chosen setting easier or harder to use.

02

Agree a narrow research question and optional ways to contribute.

03

Map current services and practical constraints without diagnosing participants.

04

Select one change within the organisation's control.

05

Return to participants and ask whether the change mattered in their terms.

The first result

A participant-approved findings brief and a measured service improvement.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

Non-clinical service research can investigate access and experience. It does not establish a mental-health treatment effect.

The open question

The source defines no population, service, validated outcome measure or qualified clinical programme.

The next measurement

Compare the participant-chosen indicator before and after the agreed organisational change.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • An experienced researcher and lived-experience advisers
  • A willing organisation with authority to change the service
  • Appropriate professional referral information

A possible business model

Commissioned research and improvement support, with personal experiences kept separate from advertising profiles.

A leadership decision to practise

Let participants influence what success means and give the organisation responsibility for acting on findings it can change.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • A simple score can hide different experiences.
  • Participants may feel pressured to give positive feedback.
  • Research may identify needs the organisation cannot meet.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Does the service reduce burdens women identify themselves?
  • Can people decline personal questions and still take part?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
