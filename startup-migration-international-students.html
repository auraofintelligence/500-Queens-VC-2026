500 Queens / Migration & International Students

Migration & International Students

Understand how newcomers find belonging, useful information and fair opportunities in Australia.

GenAI concept artwork

The need worth working on.

Migrants and international students may bring substantial skills while navigating unfamiliar services, language and social networks. A market analysis should not treat them as a single audience or assume that arrival means starting from zero. Women who have made Australia home should help define the opportunities and the questions.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop a newcomer-led research and service-design practice for one practical journey, such as finding local professional networks or understanding a community service. Use clear source-linked information and bilingual review where appropriate. Individual migration or legal questions would stay with qualified advisers.

People to build with

  • Migrant-led community organisations
  • Education providers improving non-academic support
  • Women seeking to use their skills and build local connections

A newcomer-reviewed service journey

01

Invite women with different arrival histories to choose one confusing practical process.

02

Map the information people currently use and identify gaps or contradictions.

03

Check public service details and ask qualified advisers to review any specialist boundaries.

04

Create a plain-language guide with language options chosen by participants.

05

Test the guide with new users and revise it before wider sharing.

The first result

A participant-owned findings brief and a tested navigation resource.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

Service information and lived experience can inform a bounded research project. No migration outcome or recognised qualification is promised.

The open question

The source specifies no community, institution, service partner or verified demand.

The next measurement

Check whether users can complete the chosen information task and explain their next step.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Paid migrant and student advisers
  • Language review and accessible research formats
  • Current public information and qualified specialist referral routes

A possible business model

Commissioned research and navigation resources, with community contributors sharing credit and paid roles.

A leadership decision to practise

Give newcomer advisers real decision-making power. Keep the service independent of recruitment commissions that could distort advice.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • A single language or arrival story may be treated as representative.
  • Changing service details can make guidance unreliable.
  • People may mistake general navigation for individual migration advice.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Are existing skills and leadership experience recognised?
  • Can women choose which parts of their personal story remain private?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
