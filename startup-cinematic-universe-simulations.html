Cinematic Universe Simulations

Use shared fictional worlds to explore real choices about future lives, communities and technology.

The need worth working on.

Long-term systems can be hard to discuss through technical reports alone. A simulated story world lets people experience trade-offs, notice missing viewpoints and imagine a life worth building. The challenge is to keep the story emotionally compelling while making its assumptions and invented elements visible.

What the enterprise could offer.

Create a small playable or cinematic scene in which characters face a practical future dilemma, such as sharing energy or caring for an isolated neighbour. Women of different ages, bodies, skills and styles should hold meaningful roles in the world. A companion discussion guide would connect the experience to a real decision without presenting fiction as a forecast.

People to build with

  • Film and interactive-media teams
  • Community workshop hosts
  • Learners exploring future systems

One scene, one meaningful choice

01

Choose a decision with a clear human consequence and write the perspectives of several affected characters.

02

Build a short storyboard or interactive scene using assets the team owns or has permission to use.

03

Invite a varied audience to play or watch, then discuss whose choices felt absent or constrained.

04

Revise the scene and publish an assumptions note separating observed facts from authored possibilities.

The first result

A short experience, audience feedback and a facilitator guide that can support a real discussion.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

The plan connects major storytelling with leadership development and the Grain by Grain future-resilience vision.

The open question

Whether the chosen scene improves understanding or merely reinforces the creators' existing assumptions.

The next measurement

What participants can explain afterwards, which perspectives they identify and the quality of the discussion generated.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Writer and world designer
  • Paid performers or voice talent where used
  • Accessible media production

A possible business model

Explore commissioned learning experiences, licensing to educators or a production service. Test the audience and distribution route before building a large universe.

A leadership decision to practise

Decide who has authorship, payment, credit and the ability to challenge how their community is represented.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • A beautiful world may disguise weak assumptions.
  • Interactive choices can oversimplify political trade-offs.
  • Generative assets still need rights and quality review.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Which tasks do women want help with, and which decisions or human relationships should remain in their hands?
  • How would women of different bodies, ages, abilities and working conditions test the equipment and service?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
