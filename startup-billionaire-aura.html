The need worth working on.

The original title expresses scale but does not specify an operating product. A possible research direction is an Aura workbench for people overseeing many organisations or large social commitments. The central question is how power remains accountable, not whether an AI persona can turn someone into a billionaire.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop a scenario tool for reviewing interconnected projects, obligations and impacts. Use fictional organisations to show which decisions depend on evidence, which people are affected and who has authority. Keep legal, investment and technical decisions with the relevant qualified humans.

People to build with

  • Researchers examining accountable institutional decision-making
  • Foundation or enterprise teams exploring administrative tools
  • Community representatives reviewing large-project commitments

A portfolio of promises people can inspect

01

Create a fictional collection of projects with different stakeholders and dependencies.

02

Record each commitment, decision owner and evidence source.

03

Introduce a conflict between a commercial objective and a community promise.

04

Ask different participants to use the workbench to identify the conflict and challenge a proposed decision.

05

Revise the model so unresolved impacts remain visible rather than being averaged away.

The first result

An inspectable scenario workbench and an accountability review record.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

A fictional governance prototype can test information design. It does not establish a wealth-management service or guaranteed social benefit.

The open question

The title provides no institutional client, actual portfolio, decision mandate, product or verified outcome.

The next measurement

Check whether participants can identify affected people, conflicting commitments and the human responsible for a decision.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • A governance researcher and product designer
  • Fictional institutional records
  • Participants with different stakeholder perspectives

A possible business model

Potential commissioned governance and information-design work, with any professional advisory service separately qualified and defined.

A leadership decision to practise

Decide who can inspect and contest the system, especially people affected by decisions who do not own the organisation.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • A dashboard can concentrate power while appearing transparent.
  • Aggregate scores can conceal harms to a smaller group.
  • The title can imply financial capability that the prototype does not possess.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Can women affected by a large decision challenge it without needing wealth or status?
  • Does the system make unpaid care and community impacts visible alongside money?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
