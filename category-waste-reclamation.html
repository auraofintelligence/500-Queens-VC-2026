A field of work. A shared direction.

Keep useful products and materials in service by matching a specific stream to a real need. The ten original suggestions range from computer repair and farm residues to specialised industrial and nuclear research questions. They require different expertise and must not be treated as interchangeable recovery opportunities.

A local enterprise can start with better information, repair, sorting, quality testing or logistics. The essential connection is between a responsible source operator and a customer who accepts the output. Women should have opportunities to own the service, lead technical decisions and progress through skilled work, with equipment and training that fit a broad range of bodies. Count all labour, rejected material and downstream obligations before calling the outcome circular.

Start with a useful result.

01

Secure the right relationship

Work with the responsible operator and identify one stream. A public proposal or accessible stockpile does not grant permission to handle material.

02

Ask the buyer first

Obtain an acceptance specification before purchasing equipment. Test a representative sample and document what changes between batches.

03

Prove the whole route

Follow a small authorised batch to its actual destination. Compare the result with repair, reuse and current handling, including the work and cost left behind.

Share capability. Strengthen the whole field.

What these enterprises can share

  • A material register recording source, ownership, composition, contamination, volume, seasonal variation and intended destination.
  • Relationships with qualified testing, repair and authorised handling partners suited to each stream.
  • A common costing method that includes collection, preparation, storage, transport, quality checks and material that cannot be sold.

Questions worth bringing together

  • Who does the recovery work, who owns the business and who carries responsibility for rejected material?
  • Can the tools and workflow accommodate different bodies and levels of experience?
  • Would prevention or repair create more value than processing the material again?

If equality were being designed now...

  • Whose unpaid work currently keeps materials moving, and how could value and responsibility be shared more fairly?
  • Would the collection, sorting and workshop design fit different bodies, caring schedules and transport options?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
