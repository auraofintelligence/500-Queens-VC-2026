500 Queens / Near Earth Object Mapping

Near Earth Object Mapping

Help observers turn asteroid measurements into clear, traceable understanding of objects near Earth.

The need worth working on.

A moving point in a telescope image becomes useful knowledge only when measurements, timing, uncertainty and follow-up connect. This venture could help learners or small observatories understand that process. It should contribute to established scientific practice, with any hazard assessment left to the appropriate specialist systems.

What the enterprise could offer.

Build a public-data explorer or observation-preparation service that shows how an object's estimated path changes as evidence improves. Keep original catalogue identifiers and measurement provenance visible. Women leading the project would manage scientific quality, user education and the relationship between an engaging visualisation and an honest statement of uncertainty.

People to build with

  • Astronomy educators
  • Amateur or research observatories
  • Planetary-defence learners

Follow one object's evidence trail

01

Choose a well-documented object from a recognised public catalogue and collect its observation and orbit references.

02

Create a visual timeline showing additional measurements and uncertainty rather than only a smooth orbital line.

03

Have an astronomy specialist check coordinate, unit and time handling.

04

Test whether learners understand the difference between an observation, a fitted orbit and an impact-risk assessment.

The first result

A reproducible public-data story and a checked observation-planning prototype.

The starting evidence

NASA describes an established process of discovery, follow-up measurements and orbit computation. This concept would build on that work.

The open question

Which gap an additional tool can fill for a particular observatory or learning audience.

The next measurement

Reproduction of reference values and user understanding of uncertainty and data age.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Recognised public orbit data
  • Astronomy and software expertise
  • Clear provenance records

A possible business model

Explore an educational product, commissioned visualisation or observatory workflow service, with the customer and scientific review costs identified early.

A leadership decision to practise

Decide which results the team is qualified to interpret and which require referral to established monitoring services.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • A visual close approach could be misread as an impact threat.
  • Incorrect time or coordinate conversion can corrupt results.
  • Outdated records can give false confidence.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Which tasks do women want help with, and which decisions or human relationships should remain in their hands?
  • How would women of different bodies, ages, abilities and working conditions test the equipment and service?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
