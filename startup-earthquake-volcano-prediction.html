Earthquake/Volcano Prediction

Improve hazard understanding while clearly separating earthquake prediction from monitoring and preparedness.

The need worth working on.

Communities benefit from understandable hazard information, but confident claims about the exact timing of earthquakes can mislead people. The archive combines earthquake and volcano prediction in one title; the venture must separate these different scientific problems and begin with a question qualified geoscientists consider answerable.

What the enterprise could offer.

Build a historical-data teaching or research tool that explains observations, uncertainty and official information routes. For earthquakes, frame work around hazard interpretation, research evaluation or early-warning education rather than promising exact forecasts. A volcano-focused project would need its own monitoring question, relevant observatory expertise and evidence standard.

People to build with

  • Geoscience educators
  • Emergency-planning learners
  • Researchers comparing monitoring approaches

A claim tested against a historical record

01

Choose a narrowly stated research claim and document what would count as success before inspecting test data.

02

Separate training and evaluation periods and compare the claim with a simple baseline.

03

Include false alarms, missed events and data gaps in the results.

04

Ask an independent geoscientist to review the explanation and remove language implying a public warning capability.

The first result

A reproducible evaluation, an accessible uncertainty explainer and a list of unsupported claims rejected.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Research frontier

The starting evidence

USGS states that reliable prediction of a major earthquake's time, location and magnitude has not been achieved.

The open question

Whether a proposed signal adds any out-of-sample information beyond established hazard or monitoring approaches.

The next measurement

Predefined performance on unseen records, including the cost of false reassurance and frequent false alarms.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Geoscience expertise
  • Public historical records
  • Independent statistical review

A possible business model

Educational materials or transparent research-analysis services are possible starting offers. An operational warning product would need much stronger evidence and authority.

A leadership decision to practise

Commit to publishing a null result when the proposed method fails, even if the original story is attractive.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Coincidence can be mistaken for prediction.
  • A research display could delay attention to official advice.
  • Combining different hazards can conceal invalid assumptions.

If equality were being designed now...

  • What information do women coordinating homes, care or local services need when geological conditions are uncertain?
  • Can those affected define the costs of false reassurance and repeated alerts, rather than leaving success to a model score?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
