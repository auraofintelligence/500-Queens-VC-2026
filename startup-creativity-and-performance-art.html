Creativity and Performance Art

Create paid stages and shared production resources for women with many ways of being visible.

The need worth working on.

Creative work needs more than encouragement. Artists need space, equipment, audiences, fair credit and a practical way to be paid. Representation also matters behind the camera and at the production table, where choices about whose stories and bodies belong on stage are made.

What the enterprise could offer.

Build a small artist-led production and showcase service linking performance, visual art and digital work. Keep glamorous fashion and polished spectacle alongside country style, larger bodies, older performers and nerdy experimentation. Invite artists to define their own image rather than assigning them a demographic role.

People to build with

  • Women artists and performers developing a new work
  • Community venues commissioning accessible programmes
  • Local audiences seeking distinctive creative experiences

One paid creative showcase

01

Ask a willing venue and artists to agree the audience and practical scope.

02

Create clear agreements for payment, rights, recording and access needs.

03

Offer a supported rehearsal or development session.

04

Present the work with suitable audience access and artist-approved promotion.

05

Review the event with performers, crew and audience before planning another.

The first result

A completed small showcase, properly credited works and a practical production record.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

A limited production can be planned with willing artists and a venue. No event or partnership is confirmed here.

The open question

The source names no programme, artists, venue, delivery date or committed audience.

The next measurement

Ask whether artists were fairly supported and whether the audience would attend or commission related work.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Artists, a producer and appropriate technical crew
  • A willing venue or suitable online format
  • Clear artist agreements and accessible promotion

A possible business model

Venue commissions, ticketed events or production services, with artist payment and rights included from the start.

A leadership decision to practise

Let artists lead the programme and choose how they are represented. Make backstage and technical leadership opportunities visible.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Exposure can be offered in place of fair payment.
  • Recording rights may be unclear.
  • A polished public event can conceal inaccessible rehearsals or unpaid production labour.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Can performers of different body sizes access costumes, seating and stage facilities?
  • Who is paid for organising, caring and technical work behind the performance?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
