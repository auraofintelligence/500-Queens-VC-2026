500 Queens / Just-In-Time Learning

Just-In-Time Learning

Offer useful learning at the moment someone is ready to do a real task.

The need worth working on.

A long course can be hard to fit around paid work, children, care or seasonal employment. At the same time, a short tip is not enough when a task needs supervised practice. Learners need a pathway that respects their time while being honest about what they can safely do after each step.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop small, task-based learning modules with practitioners and adult learners. Begin with a low-risk practical skill, offer text, audio and visual explanations, and connect the lesson to a piece of work the learner can show. Specialist or accredited tasks would need their own qualified teaching pathway.

People to build with

  • Adult learners returning to study or changing careers
  • Regional workers learning a new workplace tool
  • Small organisations onboarding people with varied prior experience

One task, one useful lesson

01

Ask learners and a practitioner to choose a task that causes repeated confusion.

02

Observe how people currently learn it and record the missing explanation.

03

Build a short module with a practical exercise and an offline option.

04

Watch learners attempt the task without stepping in too early.

05

Revise the material and identify when a human teacher is still required.

The first result

A tested learning module, example work and a clear next-step guide.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

A bounded adult-learning pilot can be tested with existing teaching tools.

The open question

The source defines no curriculum, learner group, teaching partner or recognised qualification.

The next measurement

Compare learners' ability to complete and explain the task before and after the module.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • A practitioner and adult-learning designer
  • Paid learner feedback
  • Accessible formats and a simple delivery platform

A possible business model

Organisation-funded onboarding or paid learning bundles, with affordable access designed alongside any commercial offer.

A leadership decision to practise

Let learners help decide the pace, format and evidence of success. Avoid measuring only how many modules they click through.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Bite-sized content can conceal the need for supervised practice.
  • An online-only design may exclude low-connectivity learners.
  • Completion badges may be mistaken for formal competence.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Can a learner pause for care or shift work without losing progress?
  • What would make this useful for someone learning in a second language?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
