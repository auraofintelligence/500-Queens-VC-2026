500 Queens / Gamification of Governance

Gamification of Governance

Make civic participation engaging while keeping real decisions accountable to the people affected.

GenAI concept artwork

The need worth working on.

Game elements can make learning and participation inviting, but points are not a substitute for voice, power or fair process. A governance tool should begin with a real decision and ask who is absent, what access they need and how participation changes the outcome. Women with little spare time should not need to compete for attention.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop a cooperative participation experience around the planning document's Scan, Design, Simulate, Do, Share sequence. Recognise learning, care and follow-through without turning recognition into money or investment rights. The tool would help a willing group understand options, contribute in different ways and see what happened to its input.

People to build with

  • Community groups
  • Local project organisers
  • Young participants and mentors

A shared-space decision people can follow

01

Choose a small decision with a willing group and state who holds authority to act.

02

Offer accessible ways to observe the need, propose options and try a simple simulation.

03

Run one agreed activity and record contribution, learning and observed results with participant permission.

04

Review whose input changed the decision and whether recognition encouraged cooperation or competition.

The first result

A tested participation flow, transparent decision record and revised recognition rules.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

The current plan defines C-Hour as recognition of verified community time with no economic equivalent, and supplies the participation sequence.

The open question

Which forms of recognition help this group participate without creating pressure, exclusion or performative scoring.

The next measurement

Participation breadth, actions completed, participant understanding of authority and useful changes made from feedback.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Willing group and decision owner
  • Accessible facilitation
  • Participant-controlled contribution records

A possible business model

Facilitation, educational tools or commissioned civic engagement may support a service. Keep financial accounts separate from contribution recognition.

A leadership decision to practise

Decide how people can influence the outcome without having the time, confidence or resources to earn the most visible recognition.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Scores can reward easy-to-count activity over care.
  • A game can create the appearance of influence without real authority.
  • Public records may expose personal circumstances.

If equality were being designed now...

  • How can a woman with little spare time still shape the decision?
  • What forms of care or quiet contribution would a points system overlook?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
