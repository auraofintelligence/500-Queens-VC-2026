## Keep one essential service working

A community facility wants to explore energy backup and does not yet have a clear service requirement.

1. Choose the essential loads and the interruption being considered.
2. Gather actual usage records and existing equipment information.
3. Compare proposed options with a qualified installer or engineer.
4. Set out maintenance, testing, responsibilities and the limits of the proposed backup.

## A useful result

A service requirement and a comparison brief ready for professional costing.

## Human review

The facility operator and qualified energy specialists review the proposed arrangement.

## Questions

- Which service matters first when supply fails?
- What evidence describes the real load?
- Who notices a fault before it becomes a failure?
- What would women in a regional community need to rely on this service and lead its operation?
