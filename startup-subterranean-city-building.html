Subterranean City Building

Break the underground-city vision into inspectable systems, human needs and decisions that can be tested.

The need worth working on.

An underground settlement would depend on geology, structure, air, water, power, access and a life people actually want. These systems cannot be made credible by drawing a large city all at once. A useful enterprise begins with a specific component or planning question and treats human experience as seriously as engineering.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop a digital systems model and a full-size surface mock-up of one underground-like space. Examine a workshop, service corridor or shared room before making claims about habitation. Link the model to maintenance access, evacuation, everyday comfort and the consequences of losing a supporting service.

People to build with

  • Infrastructure and building researchers
  • Resilience planners
  • Human-factors and access designers

One space and its supporting services

01

Choose a limited space and state who would use it, for how long and for what purpose.

02

Map every dependency, including ventilation, water, power, waste, communications and exit routes.

03

Build a surface mock-up or digital walk-through for access and maintenance review.

04

Run a service-failure exercise and publish the conditions that would make the concept unacceptable.

The first result

A dependency model, reviewed spatial brief and next-test programme for one component.

What needs to be demonstrated.

The starting evidence

The source includes autonomously built resilient cities as a long-term ambition. No inhabited underground city design is validated here.

The open question

Whether the proposed setting can provide an acceptable life and maintainable services at a defensible cost.

The next measurement

Unresolved dependencies, maintenance task access and performance of the chosen component test.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Geotechnical and building expertise
  • Human-factors and accessibility advisers
  • Integrated service modelling

A possible business model

Component design, simulation or maintenance planning may create practical enterprises before any city-scale project is justified.

A leadership decision to practise

Decide which human needs are non-negotiable and give prospective residents authority over the definition of an acceptable environment.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Interdependent systems can fail together.
  • Construction images can conceal missing life-support assumptions.
  • Long-term upkeep may be underfunded.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Which tasks do women want help with, and which decisions or human relationships should remain in their hands?
  • How would women of different bodies, ages, abilities and working conditions test the equipment and service?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
