Housing and Tourism

Study housing and visitor-economy choices together without treating residents as an afterthought.

The need worth working on.

Housing, employment and tourism can place different demands on the same spaces and services. A market study that looks only at visitor spending can miss workers' travel, residents' stability and unpaid care. A useful venture should make those trade-offs understandable before promoting a development.

What the enterprise could offer.

Create a place-based research service that compares one proposed housing or visitor service with resident and worker needs. Use current public information, clearly identified assumptions and voluntary interviews. Keep the work separate from any claim of property access, planning approval or investment return.

People to build with

  • Community groups examining a local proposal
  • Women-led hospitality or housing-service ventures
  • Organisations seeking a more complete place-based evidence brief

One place, several everyday users

01

Choose a specific service question with a willing local group.

02

Map how residents, workers and visitors currently use the relevant spaces and transport.

03

Check public records and label gaps rather than filling them with guesses.

04

Compare a limited alternative such as a service arrangement or booking approach.

05

Return the findings to the affected groups and record unresolved disagreements.

The first result

A source-linked place brief and a clearly scoped next investigation.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

A research study can compare needs and assumptions without establishing a development proposal's feasibility.

The open question

The source gives no site, ownership, permission, demand study or confirmed operator.

The next measurement

Verify the assumption that most changes the proposed service case using current records and willing participants.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • A place researcher and relevant technical advisers
  • Resident, worker and visitor perspectives
  • Reliable public records and transparent assumptions

A possible business model

Commissioned research and service-design work, with conflicts disclosed if the client has a property or operating interest.

A leadership decision to practise

Choose who helps set the research question and who reviews the findings before they are used to promote a project.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Visitor demand can be mistaken for community benefit.
  • Short observations may miss seasonal pressure.
  • A public concept can be read as a confirmed development or site agreement.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Can local women afford to live near the work and services the proposal creates?
  • Who gains stability, privacy and time, and who loses them?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
