500 Queens / Olympic Scale Entertainment

Olympic Scale Entertainment

Use the ambition of major events to design entertainment that is accessible, locally useful and responsibly delivered.

The need worth working on.

Olympic Scale Entertainment is an ambition about reach and production, not a claim of Olympic affiliation or a confirmed programme. Large events depend on everyday work: rehearsal, transport, access, toilets, rest, staffing, rights and money. A useful enterprise can begin by solving one of those problems for a willing organiser.

What the enterprise could offer.

Explore an event-design or production service that combines a memorable audience experience with practical inclusion. Start with a small performance, installation or interactive programme, designed with women of different bodies, ages, languages and caring responsibilities. Test the service in a real venue before treating scale as a success measure.

People to build with

  • Event organisers and venues
  • Artists and production workers
  • Audiences and access advisers

A small event with major-event discipline

01

Agree the purpose, audience and responsibilities with a willing host and creative team.

02

Design the audience journey from booking and arrival to seating, toilets, quiet space and departure.

03

Rehearse production, access support and disruption responses with the responsible professionals.

04

Run an appropriately authorised pilot and review the experience with audiences, artists and crew.

The first result

A tested event format, audience-access findings and an operating brief for repetition.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

The catalogue uses an Olympic-scale aspiration. No Olympic partnership, venue, date, artist or event permit is established.

The open question

Which experience audiences value enough to return to and what can scale without weakening access or working conditions.

The next measurement

Audience return interest, access barriers resolved, crew workload and delivery within the agreed resources.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Willing organiser and venue
  • Paid creative and production roles
  • Access and audience participation

A possible business model

Production fees, commissioned experiences or ticketed programmes may be tested with a defined host and realistic costs. Rights and branding require clear agreements.

A leadership decision to practise

Choose what the event should contribute to its community and protect artist and crew conditions as attendance grows.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Scale can conceal financial and staffing fragility.
  • Famous-event language can imply an unconfirmed affiliation.
  • Audience access may fail between the venue and transport home.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Can women with varied bodies and languages enjoy or work at the event without transport, toilets or care becoming barriers?
  • Who has creative and budget authority, and are women doing backstage, technical and organising work fairly paid?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
