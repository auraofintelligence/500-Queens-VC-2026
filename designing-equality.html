If equality were being designed now,

What would women want and need from the places, workplaces and systems around them?

This is a question for women to answer through their own lives. A good design begins with listening, gives people power over the choices, and tests whether daily life actually improves.

Across cities, outer suburbs, country towns, the outback and island communities, the answer can change. Australia is home to First Nations women, migrants from across the world and families with many generations here. A global network needs that same openness to different places and experiences.

Start with a life.
Then examine the system.

Ask about an ordinary day. What takes more time, money or effort than it should? Where does a woman have to adapt herself to a system that could have been designed differently? What already works well, and what should be protected?

A woman might want reliable transport, a workshop tool that fits her body, time to care for family, more influence at work, a place to feel beautiful, affordable space for an enterprise, or a different future altogether. These are examples to explore, not answers to impose.

Who is shaping the question?

Invite the women who would use, maintain, pay for, work in and live beside the proposed system. Make it possible to participate through different languages, schedules, locations and ways of communicating. Pay for substantial design work where it is part of the project.

Six ways into the bigger question.

Power and ownership

Who sets priorities, controls resources and can change a decision? Explore ownership, executive authority, board influence and the right to challenge a system.

Time and care

How do working hours, caring responsibilities, recovery, learning and time for a life fit together? Look at the whole day, including unpaid work.

Places and access

What changes between a capital city, outer suburb, regional town, remote property and island? Examine housing, transport, services, connection and physical access.

Bodies and everyday design

Whose bodies fit the furniture, clothing, equipment, vehicles and spaces? Involve women of different sizes, heights, bust sizes, ages, mobility and sensory needs.

Income and opportunity

Can women afford to take part, learn, build and lead? Ask about pay, enterprise income, training, capital, risk and routes into ownership.

Belonging and self-expression

Can women bring their language, culture, faith, style and personality? Glamorous, practical, nerdy, country, urban or something entirely their own: no single look defines a leader.

Turn the question into a practical test.

01

Listen with the women involved

Gather actual experiences, including disagreements. Ask what a better day would look like and whose experience is still missing.

02

Choose a change together

Translate a need into a design decision. Record who has authority, who bears the cost and who should benefit.

03

Try a small version

Test a timetable, space, service, tool or decision process with willing participants. Keep the first experiment useful and manageable.

04

Review what changed

Look at time, cost, access, income, safety, influence or another measure chosen by the participants. Share the result and revise the design.

Bring the question into the network.

Every enterprise brief includes questions about who it serves and how women could shape its design. The Queens bring different perspectives; the people affected supply the lived knowledge and make the decisions.
