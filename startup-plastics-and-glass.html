Plastics and Glass

Build a recovery business around clean material streams and customers who can use them.

The need worth working on.

Plastics and glass are broad material families with different collection, sorting and processing needs. A pile of discarded material is not yet a product. The venture must identify a consistent input, a customer specification and the work required to bridge the two, including transport that may be costly in regional communities.

What the enterprise could offer.

Explore one local stream such as a particular packaging type or a clean glass fraction with an authorised waste operator. Compare repair, reuse and material recovery before choosing machinery. Women could lead procurement, quality assurance and ownership design as well as collection and sorting, with physically usable equipment for varied bodies.

People to build with

  • Authorised waste operators
  • Local businesses producing a consistent stream
  • Manufacturers or reuse customers

One material, one buyer

01

Characterise a representative authorised sample by material type, contamination and available volume.

02

Ask a prospective buyer for its acceptance specification and minimum practical order.

03

Trial sorting or preparation through an appropriate existing operator, recording rejects and labour.

04

Compare revenue and avoided costs with transport, processing, quality testing and residual disposal.

The first result

A material-flow brief, buyer-tested sample and a realistic recovery decision.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

The source groups plastics and glass as a recovery theme. It supplies no verified processing method, customer or facility authority.

The open question

Whether a consistent saleable output can be produced locally at a better total outcome than current handling.

The next measurement

Accepted output, contamination, labour per unit and full delivered cost.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Authorised material access
  • A buyer specification
  • Suitable processing and quality expertise

A possible business model

Collection, preparation, reuse sales or a processing service may be viable for a particular stream. Avoid assuming all collected material has positive value.

A leadership decision to practise

Choose the stream and customer before equipment, and agree how workers benefit from safer, more productive handling.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Mixed inputs can destroy product value.
  • Freight may outweigh local processing benefits.
  • Unsold output can become a new stockpile.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Would women sorting the material be able to use the bins, benches and lifting arrangements comfortably?
  • Can those doing collection and quality work influence the sale terms and share in the value recovered?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
