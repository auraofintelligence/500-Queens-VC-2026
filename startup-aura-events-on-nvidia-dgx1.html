Aura Events on NVIDIA DGX1

Explore shared AI events through a real workload, welcoming participation and transparent computing needs.

The need worth working on.

A named computer does not define an event or its value. Participants need a useful activity, capable facilitation and a way to take part without feeling excluded by technical language. The original DGX1 title should remain part of the concept's history while equipment choices follow current requirements.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop an event and shared-computing concept around a specific creative or learning workload. Compare suitable available hardware and purchased services before committing to equipment. The pilot could demonstrate an Aura-related visualisation or collaborative AI exercise with clear human facilitation and source labels.

People to build with

  • Women exploring practical AI tools
  • Community organisations considering shared computing
  • Creative teams testing a collaborative event format

A useful AI session before a hardware purchase

01

Choose one activity participants want to complete and define its output.

02

Estimate and then measure the actual computing workload using available resources.

03

Design an accessible session with non-technical entry points and a low-bandwidth alternative.

04

Run a small supervised event using fictional or authorised material.

05

Review participant value, facilitator workload and equipment requirements together.

The first result

A completed event pilot, participant-created outputs and a measured computing brief.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

A small event can test a workload and participation format. The title does not confirm ownership, suitability or availability of a DGX1 system.

The open question

No venue, equipment, organiser, event date or computing service is committed by the source.

The next measurement

Record the resources used by the chosen task and whether participants complete the intended activity.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • An event producer and AI facilitator
  • Suitable computing access and reliable venue arrangements
  • Accessible learning materials and participant feedback

A possible business model

Commissioned events, workshops or shared-computing services, with equipment investment based on measured demand.

A leadership decision to practise

Choose the participant outcome before the machine. Let community users influence access, support hours and any shared ownership arrangement.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Hardware prestige can obscure a weak event purpose.
  • Connectivity or setup delays can exclude participants.
  • Demonstrations may imply capabilities that the actual service cannot maintain.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Can a regional participant join without expensive equipment or a long trip?
  • Are beginners, carers and non-English-first-language participants supported in the session design?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
