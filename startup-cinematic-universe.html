500 Queens / Cinematic Universe

Cinematic Universe

Create a shared story world where women shape the future as complex leads, makers and owners.

The need worth working on.

A future civilisation becomes easier to discuss when people can see everyday life inside it. But a cinematic universe can also repeat a narrow beauty ideal or make women symbolic supporters of somebody else's adventure. This concept puts varied women in the decisions that move the story.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop a women-led story studio connecting short films, illustrated scenes and interactive worldbuilding. Keep glamorous heroines alongside country women, larger women, nerdy inventors, migrants, mothers and older leaders, without turning any body type into a personality. Separate authored fiction from factual source material.

People to build with

  • Writers, filmmakers and game makers seeking a shared world
  • Community audiences exploring possible futures
  • Women wanting meaningful creative and production leadership

One future, one lived day

01

Choose one ordinary conflict in the proposed future world, such as care, ownership or access to energy.

02

Pay a small writing group to develop several characters with distinct goals and agency.

03

Produce a short scene or illustrated sequence using clearly agreed rights.

04

Screen it with the intended audience and ask what feels believable or missing.

05

Revise the story and document ownership before expanding the universe.

The first result

A finished pilot scene, character bible and agreed creative ownership record.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

A small story production can be commissioned and reviewed now; the imagined world is not a forecast.

The open question

No production company, cast, rights agreement, audience demand or distribution partner is confirmed.

The next measurement

Ask the intended audience what they understood, whose choices mattered and whether they want the next story.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Writers, performers and a production lead
  • A feasible production format and accessible screening
  • Clear rights records and paid audience consultation

A possible business model

Commissioned stories, licensed creative work or audience-supported production, with contributors' ownership agreed before commercial use.

A leadership decision to practise

Choose who has creative authority and how writers, performers and community contributors share credit and value.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Representation can become visual tokenism without character agency.
  • Shared worldbuilding can create unclear rights.
  • Ambitious scope can prevent a first finished work.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Do women of different bodies and backgrounds drive the plot and own the work?
  • Can regional creators participate without relocating?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
