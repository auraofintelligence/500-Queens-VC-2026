Global Governance Networks

Connect local decisions through transparent agreements, shared learning and accountable representation.

The need worth working on.

Groups working across places need ways to share knowledge without erasing local priorities. Large governance diagrams can hide basic unanswered questions: who may decide, whose consent is needed, how disagreements are handled and what happens when a group leaves. The opportunity is a usable coordination practice, starting with willing participants.

What the enterprise could offer.

Create a decision-record and meeting-support service for a small network of community or enterprise groups. Each group keeps its own authority while agreeing how shared work is proposed, reviewed and reported. AI may help summarise approved records, but participants must be able to correct them and see who is responsible for decisions.

People to build with

  • Community networks
  • Cooperative or enterprise groups
  • Facilitators and elected representatives

Two groups, one shared decision

01

Invite two willing groups to choose a practical shared task with a limited scope.

02

Write a plain-language agreement covering decision rights, information sharing, conflicts and withdrawal.

03

Run a facilitated decision process and record reasons, dissent and the person responsible for each action.

04

Review the experience with participants who spoke least and revise the process before adding more groups.

The first result

An agreed decision template, a completed example and a revised coordination charter.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Practical pilot

The starting evidence

The current plan proposes growth through local choice, shared capability and actual working relationships. It does not establish a global governing authority.

The open question

Whether the process is understandable and legitimate to the people expected to use it.

The next measurement

Participant understanding of authority, actions completed and unresolved objections after the decision.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • Skilled facilitation
  • Accessible meeting options
  • Participant-approved records

A possible business model

A facilitation and governance-support service could be funded by participating organisations, with clear limits on the service provider's power.

A leadership decision to practise

Choose how to protect minority voices while still allowing useful decisions and timely delivery.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Participation can become symbolic if authority is unclear.
  • Translation and access gaps may exclude people.
  • An AI summary can flatten disagreement.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Which tasks do women want help with, and which decisions or human relationships should remain in their hands?
  • How would women of different bodies, ages, abilities and working conditions test the equipment and service?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
