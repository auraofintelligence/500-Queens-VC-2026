A field of work. A shared direction.

Use a bold habitat vision to ask concrete questions about a life people would choose, the services it depends on and who decides its future. The ten original themes include preservation, excavation, maintenance, production, transport, life support, governance and entertainment. City-scale feasibility remains unproven.

Useful enterprises can emerge long before a subterranean settlement: maintainability reviews, accessible facility design, making services, participatory planning and reliable component tests. Start with one space or service and show its dependencies. Women should define what they need from privacy, bodily comfort, care, work, movement and joy, rather than being fitted into a habitat designed around an assumed standard person. Residents' ability to change the plan or leave is part of the design.

Start with a useful result.

01

Define one bounded environment

Choose a workshop, shared room or limited service model. State the purpose, time period and outside inputs instead of drawing an entire city first.

02

Ask what life should feel like

Invite varied users to name acceptable conditions, desired freedoms and unacceptable trade-offs. Keep disagreements visible rather than averaging them away.

03

Test failure and change

Examine maintenance, supply delays, service loss and adaptation. Identify a component worth testing and a clear reason to stop if the larger case fails.

Share capability. Strengthen the whole field.

What these enterprises can share

  • A transparent dependency model connecting air, water, food, energy, waste, access, communications, maintenance and human responsibilities.
  • Full-size surface mock-ups and digital scenarios for accessibility, maintenance and everyday-life review without relying on an unproven habitat.
  • Qualified engineering and human-factors mentors alongside people who would use, clean, maintain and govern the proposed spaces.

Questions worth bringing together

  • What would women want to shape from the beginning in a genuinely equal living environment?
  • Who can reject an unacceptable condition, revise a rule or choose to leave?
  • Which useful capability can be built now without claiming that the whole city is feasible?

If equality were being designed now...

  • What would women want to control about light, privacy, movement, public space and caring facilities in a new settlement?
  • Who can leave, change the design or challenge a decision, and how would that power work in practice?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
