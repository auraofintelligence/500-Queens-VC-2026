A field of work. A shared direction.

This category investigates care, learning, wellbeing, migration, disruption, food, housing, population change and governance. Its purpose is to discover a useful service question with the people affected, then test whether a proposed enterprise can answer it.

Women with lived experience, local relationships and practical service knowledge can lead rigorous research. Country communities and migrant women who have made Australia home should help define the questions, rather than being added to a standard urban survey. Good analysis can also reveal that an existing service needs support, or that no new business is the right response.

Start with a useful result.

01

Choose one journey or decision

Ask which recurring task or service problem people want changed. A narrow question makes it easier to see whether research will lead to something useful.

02

Compare records with lived experience

Check public information and speak with people doing the work or using the service. Keep differences and uncertainty visible instead of forcing them into a single average.

03

Test the implication

Return findings to participants and identify one practical experiment. Verify real availability, demand or access before describing an opportunity as an established market.

Share capability. Strengthen the whole field.

What these enterprises can share

  • Dated source records with clear definitions and gaps
  • Paid participant involvement and accessible interview formats
  • Transparent assumptions, scenarios and conflict-of-interest records
  • A route from findings to a willing organisation's actual decision

Questions worth bringing together

  • Who defines the problem and who pays for the research?
  • What evidence would change the proposed conclusion?
  • Which organisation can act on the result, and who remains excluded?

If equality were being designed now...

  • Whose unpaid time and care are missing from the analysis?
  • Can regional, disabled and multilingual participants influence the findings?
  • Does the proposal distribute ownership and bargaining power as well as services?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
