500 Queens / Starship Size Eternal Life Support

Starship Size Eternal Life Support

Investigate long-duration life support by measuring the limits of one resource loop.

The need worth working on.

The word 'eternal' expresses the source's long-term ambition. A working life-support proposal must instead describe losses, consumables, repairs, contamination and recovery from failure. People depending on a system need honest limits and maintainable equipment, not a promise of endless operation.

What the enterprise could offer.

Develop a resource-loop modelling and testing programme for a future large habitat. Begin with one non-occupied subsystem, such as a supervised water-use simulation, and connect it to a documented inventory. Keep the Starship-scale framing as a scenario, with no assumed launch or spacecraft partnership.

People to build with

  • Habitat researchers studying resource management
  • Engineers comparing maintainable recovery systems
  • Educators connecting space design with local water and energy questions

One loop with every loss recorded

01

Choose a non-occupied resource-loop question with an experienced engineer.

02

Draw each input, output, storage point and maintenance action.

03

Use measured safe test data or published reference data for an initial model.

04

Introduce a component failure and track the required intervention.

05

Have another team reconstruct the resource balance from the records.

The first result

A resource-balance model, maintenance scenario and evidence-backed list of unresolved losses.

What needs to be demonstrated.

Research frontier

The starting evidence

NASA operates and develops systems that recover water and manage cabin resources. That does not establish indefinite closed-loop life support.

The open question

The concept supplies no complete loop design, food system, repair capability or validated duration.

The next measurement

Measure or independently check the losses and consumable needs of the selected subsystem.

Build the means to deliver.

Useful capabilities and resources

  • An experienced life-support or process engineer
  • A non-occupied test arrangement or validated reference dataset
  • A clear inventory and failure-analysis method

A possible business model

Research and engineering studies could support component development, with any occupied use requiring a separate qualification programme.

A leadership decision to practise

Treat maintenance and human workload as design requirements. Make someone responsible for identifying what cannot be recovered or repaired.

People, consequences and decisions.

  • Small unrecorded losses accumulate over time.
  • One successful subsystem does not validate a complete habitat.
  • A failure may require consumables or skills absent from the proposed mission.

If equality were being designed now...

  • Whose maintenance and care labour makes the proposed habitat function?
  • Does the model accommodate varied bodies, daily routines and access needs?

Begin with women's own experiences across bodies, ages, cultures and places. Invite disagreement and choose a practical change together.
